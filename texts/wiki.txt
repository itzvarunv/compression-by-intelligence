The Boeing 747 is a wide-body airliner developed and manufactured by Boeing Commercial Airplanes in the United States. It first flew in February 1969 and entered service with Pan Am in January 1970. Its distinctive hump, formed by the raised upper deck behind the cockpit, made it one of the most recognisable aircraft ever built, and it earned the nickname "Queen of the Skies".

The aircraft was designed during the late 1960s, when airlines expected passenger numbers to grow rapidly and airports were becoming congested. A larger aircraft could carry more people on each flight and reduce the cost per seat. Boeing placed the cockpit above the main deck so that a nose cargo door could be fitted, because many engineers believed supersonic transports would soon replace subsonic airliners on passenger routes, leaving the 747 to serve mainly as a freighter.

The 747 is powered by four high-bypass turbofan engines mounted on pylons under a wing swept back at about 37.5 degrees. This sweep allows an efficient cruise speed close to Mach 0.85. Several variants were produced, including the 747-100, 747-200, 747-300, 747-400 and the stretched 747-8, which first flew in 2010. Production ended in 2022 after more than 1,500 aircraft had been delivered to airlines and cargo operators around the world.
